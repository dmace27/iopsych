### I-O Psychology

Industrial-organizational psychology is the application of psychology to work and organizations. In recruiting, that can include trying to understand characteristics associated with things like:

- job performance
- retention
- teamwork
- motivation
- leadership
- conscientiousness
- fit for a role or work environment

He mentioned something along the lines of **“drivers and depressors”**: factors that positively or negatively influence an outcome in the hiring process.

An important part of the conversation was that he has **not yet implemented his idea because of compliance concerns**. That makes sense because hiring systems involving psychological or behavioral signals have to deal with issues such as fairness, bias, validity, privacy, and employment law.

### Potential project opportunity

This was probably the strongest project lead from the event.

You could build an **explainable ATS / candidate-evaluation prototype inspired by I-O psychology**.

Rather than simply making a black-box model that assigns candidates a score, the system could explicitly show:

- what factors it considers
- which factors act as drivers or depressors
- how much each factor contributes
- why a candidate received a certain result
- fairness or bias checks
- uncertainty in the prediction
- whether certain features should even be used

The interesting part would not just be the prediction accuracy. It would be the **decision framework surrounding the prediction**.

### Data problem

Real recruiting data is generally private, so you probably cannot reproduce the exact system a large company would have.

Possible alternatives:

- public datasets from organizational psychology research
- public employee-performance / attrition datasets
- published research containing effect sizes between traits and workplace outcomes
- synthetic candidate data generated from reasonable assumptions or published research

The project should be transparent about the fact that the data is public or synthetic.
